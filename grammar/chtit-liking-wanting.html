Chtít (To Want) & Liking Things

← Back to Grammar

Chtít means "to want" — essential for ordering food, asking for things, and expressing needs. This page also covers how to say you like something.

Pattern

Chtít in the present tense

PronounChtít (to want)
já (I)chci
ty (you, informal)chceš
on / ona / ono (he / she / it)chce
my (we)chceme
vy (you, formal or plural)chcete
oni / ony (they)chtějí

Examples

  • Chci kávu.I want coffee.
  • Co chceš?What do you want?
Pattern

Saying you like something: mít rád / mít ráda

To say you like something, use mít rád (if you're a man) or mít ráda (if you're a woman) — literally "to have [something] gladly." The ending -rád / -ráda agrees with the speaker's gender, not the object being liked.

  • Mám rád čaj.I like tea. (said by a man)
  • Mám ráda čaj.I like tea. (said by a woman)

📌 Good to know

Chtěl bych (man) / Chtěla bych (woman) means "I would like" — the polite version of "chci." Use it when ordering or asking for something politely, e.g. "Chtěl bych kávu, prosím." (I would like a coffee, please.)
