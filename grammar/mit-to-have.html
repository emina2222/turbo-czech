Mít (To Have)

← Back to Grammar

Mít means "to have." You'll use it for possessions, family, time, and needs — and it's also your gateway to negation.

Pattern

Mít in the present tense

PronounMít (to have)
já (I)mám
ty (you, informal)máš
on / ona / ono (he / she / it)má
my (we)máme
vy (you, formal or plural)máte
oni / ony (they)mají

Examples

  • Mám dvě děti.I have two children.
  • Máš čas?Do you have time?
  • Ona má nový telefon.She has a new phone.
  • Nemáme peníze.We don't have money.

📌 Good to know

To make almost any verb negative, just add "ne-" to the front: mám → nemám (I have → I don't have). This same trick works for být and most other verbs too.
