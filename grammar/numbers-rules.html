Numbers & Counting Rules

← Back to Grammar

When you count things in Czech, the noun's ending changes depending on the number in front of it. There's a clear 3-tier pattern — useful for counting boxes, pallets, and hours on shift.

Pattern

The 1 / 2–4 / 5+ pattern

After 1, the noun stays singular: jedna krabice (one box).
After 2–4, the noun takes a special plural form, and "dva" (two) becomes "dvě" before feminine or neuter nouns: dvě krabice (two boxes).
After 5 and above, the noun takes the "counting form," which often looks like a shortened, bare stem: pět krabic (five boxes).

NumberKrabice (box)Hodina (hour)
1jedna krabicejedna hodina
2dvě krabicedvě hodiny
5pět krabicpět hodin
10deset krabicdeset hodin

Examples

  • Mám jednu krabici.I have one box.
  • Mám dvě krabice.I have two boxes.
  • Mám pět krabic.I have five boxes.
  • Pracuji deset hodin.I work ten hours.

📌 Good to know

This is genuinely one of the harder patterns in Czech — don't worry about perfecting it immediately. Native speakers will still understand "pět krabice" even if it's not quite right. Getting the number right matters far more than getting the ending right.
