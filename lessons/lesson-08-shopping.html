8. Shopping & Money

Shop for everyday items, ask the price, and pay in cash or by card — the phrases you'll use every time you visit a shop or grocery store.

🎯 By the end of this lesson you can:

  • Shop for everyday items
  • Ask the price
  • Pay in cash or by card
Step 1 · Vocabulary

New words

Tap the speaker to hear each word. Try repeating it out loud.

  • obchod/OB-khod/shop, store
  • obchod s potravinami/OB-khod s po-TRA-vi-na-mi/grocery store
  • peníze/PE-nyee-ze/money
  • koruna, koruny/KO-roo-na, KO-roo-ni/crown (Czech currency), crowns
  • Kolik to stojí?/KO-lik to STO-yee/How much does it cost?
  • levné/LEV-neh/cheap
  • drahé/DRA-heh/expensive
  • platit/PLA-tyit/to pay
  • hotově/HO-to-vye/in cash
  • kartou/KAR-toh/by card
  • účtenka/OOCH-ten-ka/receipt

💡 Tip

Cashiers often ask "Platíte hotově, nebo kartou?" (Are you paying in cash, or by card?) — listen for hotově or kartou and you'll know exactly what's being asked.

Step 2 · Grammar focus

Shopping phrases and numbers with money

Prices combine numbers (Lesson 2) with "korun/korun" (crowns):

CzechEnglish
Kolik to stojí? — Sto korun.How much does it cost? — One hundred crowns.
Platíte hotově, nebo kartou?Are you paying in cash, or by card?

📌 Good to know

Koruna is the Czech currency. Like the box example from Lesson 2, the word for crowns changes with the number: jedna koruna, dvě koruny, pět korun.

Step 5 · Writing

Write it yourself

Write a short exchange: ask the price of something and say you'll pay by card.

Show a sample answer
Kolik to stojí? Platím kartou.

Now translate this sentence into Czech: "This is expensive. Do you have something cheaper?"

Show a sample answer
To je drahé. Máte něco levnějšího? ("levnějšího" = "cheaper", a small step beyond A1 but useful to recognize)
