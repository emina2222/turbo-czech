Lesson 3 of 10 · ~30 minutes

Family & People

Talk about your family and the people around you — useful for small talk with new coworkers and for describing who's back home.

🎯 By the end of this lesson you can:

  • Name family members and people you know
  • Use the verb mít (to have) in the present tense
  • Use the possessives můj/moje and tvůj/tvoje
  • Talk briefly about your own family

Czech often has separate words for male and female versions of a person — like kolega and kolegyně. You'll see this pattern again and again.

Step 2 · Grammar focus

The verb mít (to have)

Mít is how you say you have something — family, kids, a colleague, anything.

PronounMít (to have)Example
já (I)mámMám dvě děti. — I have two children.
ty (you, informal)mášMáš bratra? — Do you have a brother?
on/ona/ono (he/she/it)máOna má manžela. — She has a husband.
my (we)mámeMáme tři děti. — We have three children.
vy (you, formal/plural)máteMáte rodinu? — Do you have a family?
oni/ony (they)majíMají syna. — They have a son.

📌 Good to know

Můj/moje (my) and tvůj/tvoje (your, informal) agree with the noun's gender — use můj and tvůj for masculine nouns (můj bratr), and moje and tvoje for feminine nouns (moje sestra). Example: Můj kolega je z Polska. Moje kolegyně je odsud. (My colleague (m) is from Poland. My colleague (f) is from here.)
